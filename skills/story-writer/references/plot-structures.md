# Plot structures

A working catalogue. Each entry: the beats, what it is good for, the length
it needs, and how to compress it for a short storyboard film.

## Structures built on conflict

### Three-act structure
Setup (the world, the character, what they want) → confrontation (rising
obstacles, a midpoint shift) → resolution (climax and new normal). The
default for most films. Compress to: situation, complication, turn,
resolution. Needs 45 seconds or more to breathe.

### Hero's journey (Campbell, simplified by Vogler)
Ordinary world → call to adventure → refusal → meeting the mentor →
crossing the threshold → tests, allies, enemies → approach → ordeal →
reward → the road back → resurrection → return with the elixir.
Good for transformation and quests. Needs minutes, not seconds. Compressed
to four beats: call, threshold, ordeal, return changed.

### Story circle (Dan Harmon)
You (a character in comfort) → need (they want something) → go (into the
unfamiliar) → search (adapt) → find (get it) → take (pay a price) →
return → change. A compact, more flexible hero's journey; works well at
60 to 90 seconds with one beat per step or two steps merged.

### Save the Cat (Blake Snyder)
Fifteen beats: opening image, theme stated, setup, catalyst, debate, break
into two, B story, fun and games, midpoint, bad guys close in, all is lost,
dark night of the soul, break into three, finale, final image. For
feature-length planning; for short films keep only opening image, catalyst,
midpoint, all is lost, final image. The opening and final images mirroring
each other is useful at any length.

### Freytag's pyramid
Exposition → rising action → climax → falling action → resolution
(dénouement). Classical drama and tragedy. Compresses well to five beats.

### Fichtean curve
Starts in the middle of the action with a series of crises of rising
intensity, minimal exposition, climax, brief falling action. Good for
thrillers, chases and anything that must hook in the first seconds.

### In medias res
Open on a dramatic moment, then fill in how we got there (or let the
audience infer it). A framing choice that can sit on top of another
structure. Strong for short vertical video, where the first second must hold.

## Structures without central conflict

### Kishōtenketsu (Chinese, Korean and Japanese four-part form)
Ki (introduction) → shō (development) → ten (twist: an unexpected element
or shift of view) → ketsu (reconciliation: the parts come together and the
meaning lands). No antagonist needed; the turn comes from juxtaposition.
Ideal for 15 to 30 seconds, gentle humour, quiet moments, slice of life.

### Slice of life / vignette
A moment observed, with a small change in mood or understanding rather than
plot. Works for very short films if the final image reframes the first.

## Shapes of fortune (Kurt Vonnegut's story shapes)
Plot the main character's fortune over time:
- **Man in a hole**: fine → falls into trouble → climbs out better off. The
  most reliable short shape.
- **Boy meets girl**: gets something wonderful → loses it → gets it back.
- **Cinderella**: low → rising → sudden fall → rise beyond the start.
- **From bad to worse**: keeps falling. Dark comedy, tragedy.
- **Creation story / which way is up**: ambiguous rises and falls.
Use a shape as a pacing check: where are the highs and lows, and are there
enough changes for the length?

## Booker's seven basic plots
Overcoming the monster · rags to riches · the quest · voyage and return ·
comedy (confusion resolved) · tragedy (a flaw leads to downfall) · rebirth
(a character is redeemed). These describe what a story is about; pair one
with a structure above for how it unfolds.

## Short-form patterns
- **Setup and punchline**: establish an expectation, break it. The
  backbone of comic shorts; 2 to 4 beats.
- **Reversal**: the situation flips by the end (the hunter becomes hunted,
  the gift is for someone else).
- **Twist ending (O. Henry)**: a final reveal recasts everything before it.
  Needs fair, visible clues in earlier beats.
- **Rule of three**: two repetitions set a pattern, the third breaks it.
- **Bookend / circular**: the last image mirrors the first, changed.
- **Ticking clock**: a deadline creates pace in any structure.
- **Mystery box**: open a question early, answer it late.

## Multi-view structures
- **Rashomon**: the same event from several viewpoints that disagree.
- **Parallel stories**: two lines cut together that meet or rhyme at the end.
These need length and careful continuity; avoid under 60 seconds.

## Choosing quickly
| If the story... | Try |
|---|---|
| is about one small surprise | kishōtenketsu, setup and punchline |
| has a problem the character solves | man in a hole, three acts |
| changes who the character is | story circle, hero's journey |
| must hook instantly | in medias res, Fichtean curve |
| ends on a reveal | twist ending, mystery box |
| is funny | setup and punchline, rule of three, reversal |
